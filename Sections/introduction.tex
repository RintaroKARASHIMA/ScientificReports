In the twenty first century, international competition has significantly intensified.
Countries worldwide formulate diverse industrial policies aimed at enhancing competitiveness.
Key technological fields form the environment from which innovation arises.
Identifying key technological fields is critical for national competitiveness.
However, accurately pinpointing key technological fields remains challenging.
This difficulty arises because innovation emerges from a complex system involving countries, cities, regions, corporations and technologies \cite{weaver1948}.

Policymakers still lack a clear data driven understanding of technologies and corporations that hold rare and sophisticated capabilities essential for growth.
This study uses approximately 2.6 million Japanese patent records to identify such technologies and actors.
Technological complexity at the corporate level is quantified for the first time.

Globally, different regions and institutions approach this challenge from various perspectives.
In the European Union, the concept of Smart Specialisation helps identify key domains for policy support \cite{EC_SSP_nodate,Teixeira2022}.
In Japan, science and technology strategies continue to evolve \cite{NISTEP_nodate}.
Various indicators are proposed for assessing innovation across manufacturing, services and advanced materials \cite{Taques2021,Farcal2023}.
Studies typically focus on national level \cite{Teixeira2022,Abay2024}, regional level \cite{Balland2018,PintarScherngell2022,Pinheiro2022,Whittle2019} or corporate level analyses \cite{Bruno2018,Buccellato2016,Kito2018}.
Recent research explores diverse sectors such as Industry 4.0 \cite{Teixeira2022}, pharmaceutical biotechnology \cite{Sakakibara2014,Nakamura2022,Bhatia2018,Murakami2024} and service innovation \cite{Taques2021}.

Among these approaches, the Hidalgo Hausmann algorithm is notable \cite{Hidalgo2009eci}.
Hidalgo and Hausmann use trade data to represent productive capabilities linking countries and products as a bipartite network.
Their Product Complexity Index quantifies complexity by iteratively calculating capability diversity of countries and rarity required by products.
The Hidalgo Hausmann algorithm has been adapted to various contexts including economic analyses, patents and technological capabilities \cite{Hidalgo2021,Hidalgo2023,Hausmann2024,Balland2022,Chakraborty2020}.
True key technologies cannot be recognised by examining isolated fields because their importance arises from interdependencies.

Building on this foundation, Balland and Rigby propose the Technological Complexity Index to measure technological fields using patent data \cite{Balland2017tci}.
The Technological Complexity Index captures interplay between ubiquity and sophistication \cite{Balland2018}.
Complementary metrics include fitness based approaches \cite{Tacchella2012,Wu2016,Albeaik2017} and frameworks for classification and measurement \cite{Schmoch2008,Soete1987,Balassa1965,Mealy2019,PintarEssletzbichler2022}.
These approaches recently extend to Asian contexts and corporate level analyses due to increasing data availability \cite{Jun2023,Dong2021}.
However, comprehensive application of these methods in Japan remains limited.
Japan experiences prolonged economic stagnation often termed the Lost Decades \cite{Kobayashi2024}.
Despite related corporate analyses such as automotive suppliers \cite{Kito2018}, broad analysis of Japanese technological capabilities through complexity is notably absent.

This study addresses this gap by applying the Hidalgo Hausmann algorithm to patent data from the Japan Patent Office.
A bipartite network links corporations to technological fields and projects to a monopartite network to calculate the Technological Complexity Index.
The index represented by the second largest eigenvector of the adjacency matrix assesses technological spillovers and rarity simultaneously.
Temporal analysis from 1981 to 2010 demonstrates robust depiction of technological capabilities beyond simple patent counts.
The analysis details technological capabilities evolution over time.

This study contributes significantly to understanding innovation ecosystems through complexity.
Findings inform targeted effective innovation policies and strategies.
