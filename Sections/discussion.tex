\section*{Discussion}
\phantomsection
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Discussion}
This study applies the Technological Complexity Index (TCI) to Japanese corporate patent data across various technological fields to identify sophisticated areas critical to innovation.
Accurate assessment of technological complexity is essential for policymakers and corporate strategists aiming to maintain competitive advantages amidst intensifying global competition.
Previous regional approaches have struggled with instability when employing detailed technological classifications, resulting in coarse and potentially misleading insights.
This research provides three primary contributions.
First, it demonstrates methodological robustness at the corporate level.
Unlike regional approaches, corporate-level TCI retains stable technological rankings even when classifications become highly detailed.
Our results reveal notable complexity in the Pharmaceuticals and Chemistry sectors, likely reflecting Japan's industrial focus during the pharmaceutical industry's formative period.
This consistency across classification scales represents significant progress compared to previous regional analyses that lost reliability when switching from broader to finer classifications.
Second, the empirical findings underscore the advantage of evaluating technological complexity at the corporate level.
Prior research suggested that complexity rankings could converge due to methodological limitations of classification systems~\cite{Hidalgo2021}.
However, our corporate-level analysis maintains clear distinctions between technological fields even at granular classification levels.
This evidence highlights the corporate approach’s potential to provide deeper and more accurate insights into technological dynamics within specific industries or regions.
Third, our results offer practical implications for industrial policy and strategy.
The identification of high-complexity technological sectors informs targeted investment and innovation strategies, especially relevant amid rapid advances in artificial intelligence and growing global competition.
Corporate strategists can leverage these insights to allocate resources effectively, fostering technological leadership and competitive differentiation.
Nevertheless, our study faces several limitations.
Capturing dynamic shifts in technological trends requires a balance between long-term analysis, which ensures stability, and short-term assessments, which detect emerging innovations.
Shortening aggregation periods introduces challenges due to the sensitivity of TCI calculations to sparse patent data, thus decreasing result stability~\cite{PintarEssletzbichler2022}.
The HH algorithm underlying TCI calculations evaluates complexity based on node connections within a bipartite network, making it vulnerable to inaccuracies when patent data are sparse.
To address these challenges, future studies could integrate advanced methods such as the Fitness Complexity algorithm~\cite{Tacchella2012}, which employs nonlinear iterative processes to better capture network structures, enhancing robustness and reliability~\cite{Wu2016,Albeaik2017}.
Additionally, weighting links by citation counts rather than raw patent numbers could offer a more precise representation of technological effort and impact.
Future research directions include comparative robustness analyses of TCI with alternative indicators such as patent citations, application of machine learning techniques to predict technological shifts, and cross-national comparative studies.
Such explorations will deepen our understanding of technological complexity and provide richer guidance for industrial development strategies.
Ultimately, this research significantly advances our understanding of technological complexity at the corporate level in Japan.
By clearly identifying key technology sectors, our findings inform strategic planning and policy-making, positioning Japan advantageously in an era marked by technological acceleration and intensified international competition.
